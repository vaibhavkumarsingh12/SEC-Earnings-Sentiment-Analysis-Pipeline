The Market Reaction FinBERT vs CAR

Our first investigation sought to determine if the complex, dense narratives within SEC filings immediately move the needle on stock prices. We mapped the deep-learning generated FinBERT Net Sentiment against Cumulative Abnormal Returns (CAR), tracked over a [-1, +3] day trading window relative to the XLF sector benchmark.

Financial Insight: Short-term stock returns do not show a significant linear correlation with dense MD&A narrative sentiments. The market digests deep regulatory risk disclosures over a wider horizon rather than in immediate trading windows.

Statistics: Pearson r = -0.092 (p = 0.6621) | Spearman rho = +0.049 (p = 0.8152)
Scatter plot with linear regression and confidence bands illustrating the flat relationship between immediate market returns and text sentiment.

The Fundamental Truth Key Discovery

If immediate stock prices don't reflect the sentiment in the text, what does? We aligned the FinBERT narrative sentiment scores with the quarterly Earnings Per Share (EPS) Surprise %. The results were astounding.

Financial Insight: We discovered a strong, statistically significant rank-order monotonic correlation between context-aware FinBERT sentiment and core fundamental EPS surprises! Firms displaying positive sentiment momentum in narrative discussions are highly correlated with stronger corporate outperformance on earnings surprises.

Statistics: Pearson r = -0.009 (p = 0.9716) | Spearman rho = +0.524 (p = 0.0307)
Plot mapping deep learning sentiment directly to fundamental EPS surprises. Notice the monotonic upward trend captured by the Spearman rank correlation.

Deep Learning vs Legacy Lexicons Model Comparison

To prove the value of our Transformer architecture (FinBERT), we benchmarked it against the traditional Loughran-McDonald (LM) financial lexicon—a classic "bag-of-words" counting method used in quantitative finance for over a decade.

Financial Insight: The traditional lexicon counting shows a directional positive correlation with fundamental surprises but fails to cross the 95% statistical significance threshold. This highlights the superior analytical power of transformer-based deep learning over static word-counting engines when interpreting complex disclosures.

Statistics (LM vs EPS): Pearson r = +0.023 (p = 0.9300) | Spearman rho = +0.400 (p = 0.1121)
